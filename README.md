Australian Consumer Payment Behaviour | 2007–2022


An independent Power BI portfolio project analysing how Australian consumers' payment methods changed across six Reserve Bank of Australia Consumer Payments Surveys.
Project objective
Explore changes in cash, debit card and credit card usage, and investigate variation by age group, household income, region, transaction size and payment purpose.
Tools and methods
- Power BI: interactive dashboards, KPI cards, slicers and page navigation
- Power Query: data preparation, unpivoting survey-year columns, data types and missing-value handling
- DAX: calculation of payment shares and percentage-point changes
- Excel: source dataset format

Selected findings
- Cash's share of transactions by number fell from approximately 69.0% in 2007 to 13.3% in 2022.
- Debit cards accounted for approximately 50.5% of transactions in 2022, and exceeded cash in the 2019 survey after trailing it in 2016.
- Cash payment share declined across all surveyed age groups. The difference between ages 65+ and 18–29 widened from approximately 11.2 percentage points in 2007 to 20.6 percentage points in 2022.
- For transactions of $1–$10, the cash share fell from approximately 95.3% in 2007 to 22.9% in 2022.

Interpretation and limitations
- The workbook contains aggregated survey statistics, not individual customer transactions.
- The years are discrete survey observations: 2007, 2010, 2013, 2016, 2019 and 2022. The analysis cannot identify exact crossover dates between surveys.
- Payment shares are reported as percentages on a 0–100 scale. Changes are expressed in percentage points.
- Findings describe observed associations and trends, not causal effects.
- This is an independent portfolio analysis, not an RBA-endorsed publication.
